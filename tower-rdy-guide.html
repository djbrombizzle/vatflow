What are you working?

TMU — run the FCA

You own the program: draw/enable the line, set scope and rate, choose auto or manual sequencing, and keep the sequence honest when traffic outside your ARTCC is not on VATFLOW.

Your home tool

FCA Builder for create / edit / sequence / enable. Watch field RDY on Airport TMU Departures / My Dashboard; use Release Board for the Center-wide picture. Staff setup: FCA How-To.

Auto vs manual sequencing

This is the biggest TMU decision after the line is live. It changes who “owns” order — the ETA engine, or you.

AUTO (default)

Airborne by ETA (fixed). Unready ground is advisory only and holds no slot. The first aircraft you RDY claims the earliest open gap.

  • Use for normal metering.
  • Best when pilots call ready in a sensible order.
  • Leave here unless you need to force a sequence.

MANUAL

Drag a strip → manualSeq locks your order. Times chain along your sequence, not the global earliest gap. UI shows ✋ MANUAL · ↺ auto (Builder) or AUTO to return.

  • Use to force taxi / runway / coordination order.
  • Example: keep A before B even if B’s ETA is earlier.
  • Return to auto when the special order is no longer needed.
When to leave it in AUTO

Default for most events. Ready aircraft naturally fill gaps; unready projections do not steal slots. Less babysitting, fewer “why did my time jump?” moments when someone RDYs out of taxi order.

When to go MANUAL

Go manual only when you must enforce an order the ETA engine will not: runway config, gate preference, a specific tower request, or protecting a priority flight. Tell the positions you flipped — they will see different proposed times. Click ↺ auto / AUTO when done so the board does not stay stuck in a stale hand order.

Scope — who gets metered

  • ARTCC tag (owning ARTCC) — who may edit the program. Access control only.
  • Scope (ARTCCs) — which aircraft are included in metering. Blank = everywhere the route crosses the line. Listed centers = only aircraft inside those FIR boundaries (ground judged at their departure field; air at present position).
Why scope matters

A tight scope keeps your sequence about your problem set. A blank or wide scope pulls in distant departures that cross the line on paper — including traffic you cannot see issuing CFRs. Match scope to the centers that should actually participate in the program.

Traffic outside your airspace (not on VATFLOW)

VATFLOW only knows aircraft that appear in the VATSIM feed and match your FCA filters/scope. Controllers upstream or in an adjacent ARTCC may not be issuing RDY at all.

  • Airborne is fixed. Jets already flying toward the line keep their ETA. You cannot “delay” them in the tool — conflicts between air are flagged, not rewritten.
  • Watch the air picture (vATIS/vNAS/VATSpy/map layers) for aircraft that will cross your line without a VATFLOW release. They still consume spacing in the real world.
  • Do not assume every ground strip will RDY. If a neighboring facility is not using the board, their departures may roll when ready and show up as air — your frozen releases can only bump later when air encroaches.
  • Coordinate early with adjacent TMU/Center: share the FCA name, scope, and whether you are in AUTO or MANUAL. One shared sequence beats two parallel guesses.
  • Rate / MIT honesty: if a large share of the stream is outside VATFLOW, the in-tool sequence understates demand — tighten verbally or widen participation, don’t just stare at green RLSD times.

Center — protect the stream

You work the ARTCC-wide Release Board — the best view for Center. Airborne crossings are fixed; your job is stream awareness, coordinating with tower/APP (who issue from Airport TMU), and not inventing delay the engine cannot apply to air.

Your home tool

Release Board — select your ARTCC, load the filter, sign in on position. DEL/GND/TWR/APP manage their field on Airport TMU Departures / My Dashboard; same FCA releases sync everywhere. Programs are built by TMU in FCA Builder.

Mental model

  • AIR strips — already flying; ETA to the line is fixed. Spacing problems between air are flags for you, not times the tool rewrites.
  • CFR / advisory ground — projected only. They hold no slot until someone presses RDY.
  • RLSD — frozen wheels-up. That departure owns a crossing slot. Cancel (RLSD ✕) returns them to advisory.

Center best practices

  • Don’t RDY a ground strip “just in case” — advisory projections free the slot for someone who is truly ready.
  • If TMU is in MANUAL, honor that order; don’t fight it with random RDY timing.
  • Optional HHMMz + SET before RDY when the pilot quotes a later wheels-up — never issues before that floor.
  • Full control (ARTCC editor) can drag-reorder / return to AUTO — only do that with TMU awareness.
  • One voice with the towers on a stream beats three people issuing the same callsigns — let the field own Departures / My Dashboard RDY when they can.

APP / TWR — issue the release

DEL, GND, TWR, and APP: manage FCA times for your departures on Airport TMU. You do not need to build the program — find rows with FCA PRGM and lock Ready Time / RDY there. Leave the ARTCC-wide Release Board to Center.

Your home tool

Airport TMU → Departures (one tower field) or My Dashboard (your personal airport / ARTCC / approach list). When FCA PRGM is set, Ready Time and RDY go to the FCA engine and sync with Builder and Release Board.

What the times mean

FCA PRGM — this departure is in an FCA; that program owns the release (not destination AAR).
Ready Time — pilot’s earliest wheels-up floor (HHMMz).
RDY / RLSD — frozen wheels-up for the FCA crossing. Until you press RDY, the time is only a projection (advisory).

Why one airplane is earlier than another

  • Closer / earlier ETA to the FCA line.
  • Someone pressed RDY on them first (they claimed the open gap).
  • TMU put the sequence in MANUAL and dragged them higher.
  • Rate / MIT spacing ahead of the next aircraft.
  • They have an earlier (or no) Ready Time floor.

How to issue

  1. 1

    Open Airport TMU for your field

    Departures — set your tower ICAO in the header for one field.
    My Dashboard — add the airports (or ARTCC / approach sectors) you are working so all pending ground departures show in one list.

    Airport TMU · Departures · My Dashboard
  2. 2

    Find FCA rows

    Look for FCA PRGM populated. Those rows use FCA metering — Ready Time and RDY there freeze the FCA release.

    Column · FCA PRGM
  3. 3

    Ask the pilot

    When they call ready — or quote a later wheels-up — decide: next open slot, or a floor time.

    Pilot ready call
  4. 4

    Optional Ready Time floor

    Enter HHMMz in READY TIME → Set. The release will never be before that time; spacing may still push later.

    READY TIME · HHMMz · Set
  5. 5

    Press RDY

    Use RDY now (or Set after a Ready Time) to lock wheels-up. Tell the pilot that time. One-click RDY without a floor uses the ready-now buffer (~3 min).

    CALL FOR RELEASE · RDY now
  6. 6

    Fix mistakes

    Cancel with × to return to advisory if they aren’t ready or you issued the wrong callsign. Re-RDY when they actually are.

    Cancel · re-issue

APP / TWR best practices

  • Work Departures / My Dashboard — not the Release Board. Center owns the ARTCC-wide board.
  • Don’t RDY before the pilot is ready. Advisory rows don’t hold slots — freezing early steals from someone who can go.
  • Issue in taxi / runway order when you can. If TMU is in MANUAL, follow that order.
  • Use the Ready Time floor when the pilot says they’ll need 10+ minutes — don’t lock a “now” slot they’ll miss.
  • One position owns RDY for a gate/stream when possible (usually GND/TWR). Don’t double-issue with Center.
  • After cancel, times for others may improve — re-check the list before you brief the next pilot.
  • Only treat FCA PRGM rows as FCA releases. No program / VFR / ground stop rows follow their own rules.
